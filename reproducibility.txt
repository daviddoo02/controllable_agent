Reproducibility
---------------

To evaluate the baselines presented in Touati & Ollivier (2021) and Eysenbach et al. (2019),
experiments were conducted using the publicly available controllable_agent repository by Meta
Research, which provides unified implementations of both the Forward-Backward (FB) agent and
the Diversity Is All You Need (DIAYN) agent within a shared training framework. To ensure consistency across machines, a shared conda environment was used by all team members,
with PyTorch 2.4.1 and CUDA 12.1. Experiments were distributed across multiple machines
including local workstations [TEAMMATE SPECS HERE] and Google Colab instances.

The original papers differ from this reproduction in their choice of environments. Touati &
Ollivier (2021) evaluated the FB agent on discrete and continuous maze navigation, FetchReach,
and Ms. Pacman, while Eysenbach et al. (2019) evaluated DIAYN primarily on MuJoCo locomotion
tasks including HalfCheetah, Hopper, and Ant. In this reproduction, both agents are evaluated
on the MuJoCo continuous control tasks available in the controllable_agent repository, namely
cheetah_run and walker_walk. Although the exact environments do not match those in the original
papers, cheetah_run serves as the closest analogue to the HalfCheetah task used by Eysenbach
et al. (2019), and walker_walk provides a common benchmark for comparing both agents under
identical conditions.

Each agent was trained for 2 million environment frames, compared to approximately 1 million
steps used in the original papers. This deviation was intentional, as the MuJoCo tasks in this
repository are higher-dimensional and more complex than several of the original benchmark
environments, requiring additional training to observe meaningful learning behavior. All runs
used the default hyperparameters provided by the repository. To account for variance, each
agent-task combination was run with three random seeds (seeds 1, 2, and 3), consistent with
the evaluation protocol of Touati & Ollivier (2021). Training was monitored using TensorBoard,
with evaluation episodes logged every 10,000 frames and model snapshots saved at fixed
intervals throughout training.

It is important to note that this reproduction does not constitute a direct numerical
replication of the original results, given the differences in environments and training
duration. Rather, it aims to reproduce the relative performance trends reported in the papers —
specifically, whether FB demonstrates more stable and higher downstream task performance
compared to DIAYN — using the closest available experimental setup.
